Project proposal: ApplySafe

A Canada-first job-scam detection tool for international students

Muhammad Umer Aamir · MAC, Wilfrid Laurier University (Brantford) · 6 October 2026
My own independent project — comments only, not supervision. GitHub: github.com/umeraamir69 · applysafe · related: secsentry, Urdu text-reuse demo

1. The problem

International students and PGWP holders in Ontario are a documented target of employment and immigration-adjacent fraud. Harm includes early requests for a SIN, passport, or void cheque on Telegram/WhatsApp; Interac-in / crypto-out mule and counterfeit-cheque patterns (Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre); worker-paid “LMIA / guaranteed job / PR” schemes; and crypto “task / boost” jobs that impersonate real Canadian firms (CAFC, May 2024).

IRCC and campus offices already publish checklists. Students do not open a PDF while a recruiter is waiting in chat. ChatGPT is a weak control: it is not on the job page, it is not tested, it misses Canada-specific terms (SIN, Interac, LMIA), it leaks the message, and a formal header or “signature” often makes it call the offer legitimate.

Gap: a small, explainable, Canada-specific tool that highlights why a message is risky and can be evaluated on a public set.

2. What I propose to build

ApplySafe — paste a posting or recruiter message (or scan the current tab) → scam / risky / unclear / looks-ok, with triggering phrases highlighted.

LayerRole
Rule engineJudge. Public CAFC/IRCC/Settlement.org flags. Hard rules cannot be overridden by a model.
BrainTF–IDF + logistic regression for paraphrases. May lift unclear → risky only. Not ChatGPT.
EvidencePer-scan: domain age (RDAP), email vs claimed brand, one GET to a careers URL the user typed. Not a LinkedIn scrape.
API + websitePOST /v1/scan; Next.js paste UI; privacy page; disclaimer.
Chrome (MV3)User-gesture scan on Indeed.ca / LinkedIn. Rules on device.
ApplySafe-CA500 synthetic Canadian texts, published (GitHub + Hugging Face, CC BY 4.0).

Not: legal/IRCC advice; work-permit eligibility; bulk job-board scraping; an LLM as the verdict. A new domain is a signal, not “the company is fake.”

3. What we get if it works

  • Students: a warning in the path (page / paste box), not a poster.
  • MAC: a public Canadian artefact (URL, API, dataset, write-up). Optional campus workshop after a privacy check.
  • Learning: tested rules, small model, API, extension, published data — next to SecSentry and the Urdu demo.
  • Research (modest): a labelled synthetic benchmark and an eval table (rules vs brain vs mix vs a frozen ChatGPT pass on 50 holdout rows). Not a claim of SOTA.

4. How realistic is this?

Realistic as a part-time term build. I am not proposing real victim chats or a production anti-fraud platform.

In favourConstraint
Rules map to public government textPhrases go stale — that is why a small brain exists
500 synthetic rows is a writing taskMust not copy official pages or live ads
EMSCAD (~18k, 2012–14) for a baseline comparisonNot Canadian; I will say so
Next.js, sklearn, Chrome MV3 are familiarStore review can lag; unpacked GitHub zip is the fallback

Success bar: 500-row public set; live site; working API; holdout eval table; extension submitted or unpacked. I will not call it “AI that detects all scams.”

5. Ethics

Disclaimer on every result; report to CAFC (1-888-495-8501). No ID images or raw SIN in storage; logs are a hash of the text. Dataset is synthetic so it can be published. If offered to Laurier students, I will treat it as a personal tool until advised on privacy/ethics.
